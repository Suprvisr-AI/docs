# Collaboration

Cumulus chat sessions aren't single-player. You can invite teammates into any session, see each other's messages in real time, and work with (or without) the AI in the same thread.

---

## Adding people to a session

Open a chat and look for the **member chips** along the top of the conversation. Click the **+** chip to add a teammate from your organization. When someone joins or leaves, Cumulus posts a system message in the timeline so everyone knows who's in the thread.

The session creator is marked with an **OWNER** badge on their chip.

---

## @-mentions

Type `@name` anywhere in a message to mention a teammate. If they aren't already in the session, Cumulus automatically adds them — so you don't have to go through the member chips first. This is the fastest way to pull someone into an in-progress thread.

---

## AI Mute

Sometimes you want teammates to talk to each other in the same thread without the AI replying to every message. The **AI Mute** toggle suppresses AI responses for the session. Turn it back off whenever you're ready for the AI to chime in again.

<Callout type="tip">
  Use AI Mute when you want to coordinate with a teammate in context — planning your next prompt, reviewing a result together, or deciding how to proceed — before re-engaging the AI.
</Callout>

---

## Leaving a session

Any participant who isn't the session owner can leave via the **chip menu** — click their own chip and select **Leave**. The owner's session is unaffected.

---

## Unread indicators

Chat rows in the sidebar show an **unread dot** when a teammate has posted since you last viewed the session. Open the chat to clear it.

---

## Project sessions vs. standalone chats

Collaboration works in both project sessions and standalone chats. In a project session, the project's members are already visible to everyone in the thread.

If you started a standalone chat and later want to bring a project's shared context and members along, you can promote it into a project — see [Conversations](/features/conversations) for the move flow.
